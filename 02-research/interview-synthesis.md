# Interview Synthesis — Streakly User Research

*Source: 3 user interviews — Priya S. (power user, 14-month streak), Tom R. (churned, broke a 12-day streak at week 5), Amara L. (new user, 4 days in).*

## Top 5 Themes

**1. Streak progress is a source of pride and identity, not just a counter**
> "The thing that made it stick was hitting a 30-day streak and the app actually celebrating it, that little moment of 'look what you built.'" — Priya
> "I got to a 12-day streak and I was proud of it." — Tom

**2. The all-or-nothing reset is experienced as punishment, with no way back**
> "Then I traveled for work, missed two days, and came back to a big fat zero." — Tom
> "There was no way to recover it, nothing. So I gave up." — Tom
*(Only Tom describes going through an actual break — no second respondent to corroborate, but it's the most direct account of the mechanic in the data.)*

**3. Streak-break notification tone makes the moment worse, not better**
> "The app sent me this 'you lost your streak' notification that just made me feel bad." — Tom
*(Only one quote available — no other respondent went through a break to compare against.)*

**4. Streak anxiety starts before any break ever happens**
> "I'm already stressed about the streak, I keep thinking, what happens if I miss a day?" — Amara
> "I don't want to lose everything I've built after four days." — Amara

**5. Competitor mechanics (streak freeze) are the explicit bar users compare against**
> "I switched to Duolingo, at least there a missed day doesn't wipe everything, and a streak freeze feels forgiving." — Tom
*(Only one quote available.)*

## Contradictions / Tensions

- **Same mechanic, opposite reactions depending on outcome:** Priya credits the streak/celebration mechanic as *the reason it stuck* for her; Tom and Amara experience that same all-or-nothing design as the reason to dread or quit the app. The mechanic isn't uniformly bad — it's bad specifically for anyone approaching or crossing a break.
- **Churn isn't confined to the earliest days:** Priya frames early quitting as expected ("most people quit long before the habit forms," ~3 weeks to stick). But Tom churned at week 5, after building a real 12-day streak — well past Priya's 3-week formation window. This suggests the reset/no-recovery problem can hit users who've already invested real time, not just brand-new users.

## Single Most Important Insight for Week-1 Retention

Amara — the only respondent still in week 1 — is already anxious about losing her streak on day 4, *before any break has occurred*. That suggests the all-or-nothing mechanic may be suppressing week-1 engagement on its own, independent of whether a user ever actually breaks a streak. A comeback-after-break flow (like the one Lena sketched) addresses Tom's churn moment, but it may do little for week-1 retention specifically — because the damage there is anticipatory. For week-1, the more relevant fix may be signaling forgiveness *upfront* (e.g., a visible streak-freeze or "your progress is safe" cue before day 4–7), not only after a miss.
